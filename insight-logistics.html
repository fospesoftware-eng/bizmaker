REAL ASSETS — SEPTEMBER 2025

The institutional case for logistics & industrial assets.

Logistics has become one of the most durable real-asset classes in the UAE. But the spread between institutional-quality space and commodity warehousing is widening — and the underwriting has to reflect it.

For a long time, logistics in the UAE was treated as a yield trade: buy the shed, collect the rent, sell to the next yield buyer. That model still exists, but it is no longer the whole market. The assets attracting institutional capital are being underwritten as operating businesses — with specification, location and tenant utility doing as much work as the capitalisation rate.

From yield trade to operating asset

The demand drivers are familiar: e-commerce fulfilment, regional distribution, food security, pharmaceutical cold chain and the UAE's role as a re-export hub. What has changed is the sophistication of the occupier. Large tenants now compare buildings on throughput, not only rent.

That changes the owner's job. A warehouse is not a passive box. It has to work for the operation inside it — and the cost of it not working shows up as shorter leases, higher incentives and weaker renewal probability.

The specification premium

Clear height, floor loading, yard depth, truck circulation, dock levellers, fire compliance and power capacity are not technical footnotes. They determine which occupiers can use the building and how much they will pay to stay.

Older stock with low clear height and constrained yards can still trade, but the buyer pool narrows. The asset becomes a specialist play on low rent rather than a core income asset. That is a legitimate strategy — but it should be priced as one.

The spread between a well-specified shed and a compromised one is widening. Clear height and power are not details.

Location is a network

In logistics, location is not a point on a map. It is the combination of port access, highway connectivity, aviation and last-mile reach, labour catchments and — increasingly — power. Jebel Ali, Dubai South, National Industries Park and the Abu Dhabi–Dubai corridor each offer a different network position, and each suits a different occupier.

We underwrite the journey, not the postcode: how a truck enters and leaves the site at peak, where the workforce comes from, and what happens when the surrounding infrastructure changes.

What can go wrong

  • Supply pipeline. A strong rent in a submarket with committed competing supply can be a short-lived rent.
  • Obsolescence. A building that meets today's specification may not meet the next tenant's requirement for automation, height or power.
  • Energy cost. Temperature-controlled space is exposed to power pricing and efficiency. The service charge is part of the occupational cost.
  • Tenant concentration. A single dominant occupier can make the income look stable until it is not.

What we look for

Our checklist is deliberately physical before it is financial:

  • Clear height and floor loading against the target occupier set.
  • Yard depth and circulation for the expected vehicle profile.
  • Power capacity and the cost of upgrading it.
  • Fire compliance and the capex required to meet current standards.
  • Lease structure, escalation, reinstatement and renewal economics.
  • Exit buyer pool: who will want this asset in five to seven years, and why?

Logistics remains one of the most compelling real-asset classes in the region. The case is stronger, not weaker, when the asset is underwritten as an operating product rather than a bond with a roof.
