LAND & DEVELOPMENT — SEPTEMBER 2025

Land values follow infrastructure. Read the corridor.

Infrastructure announcements move land values before planning catches up. The opportunity is in reading the sequence correctly — and underwriting the time between promise and delivery.

LAND & DEVELOPMENT SEPTEMBER 2025 6 MIN READ RASHID AL MANSOORI · ADVISORY

In this note

Infrastructure precedes price Three corridors worth reading Planning is a timing signal The underwriting discipline

Related

Land is the most narrative-driven asset in real estate. A road, a rail line, a port expansion or a new power substation can reset expectations long before anything is built. The trap is to buy the story rather than the sequence — and to pay today for value that may not arrive for a decade.

Infrastructure precedes price

Land values do not move uniformly with announcements. They move in stages: first on expectation, then on approval, then on construction, and finally on operation. Each stage removes a different risk — and each stage attracts a different buyer.

The early buyer is taking planning and delivery risk for a lower entry price. The later buyer is paying for certainty. The skill is to know which risk you are actually underwriting, and whether the price already reflects the stage you are buying.

The opportunity is not in reacting to the announcement. It is in reading the sequence of delivery and underwriting the time between promise and operation.

Three corridors worth reading

Dubai South and the aviation–logistics axis. The combination of airport, logistics and planned urban growth gives this corridor a genuine multi-demand base. The underwriting question is absorption: how much industrial and mixed-use product can the corridor support at the same time?

The Abu Dhabi–Dubai digital and industrial corridor. Power, fibre and land availability are pulling data centres and advanced industry outward. Sites that can secure energisation early are fundamentally different from sites that cannot.

The northern Emirates. Lower land cost, new infrastructure and tourism investment are creating opportunities in hospitality, logistics and residential. The risk is thinner liquidity and a smaller buyer pool at exit.

Planning is a timing signal

Planning is not a binary. Masterplan adoption, zoning change, plot subdivision, utility allocation and building permits are separate steps, often controlled by different entities. A site can be “master-planned” for years without being developable on the timetable the buyer assumes.

We map the approval chain before we underwrite the value. The most common mistake in land is not mispricing the end value; it is mispricing the time and cost required to reach it.

The underwriting discipline

  • Infrastructure delivery date. Who is delivering it, under what contract, and what happens if it slips?
  • Utility capacity. Power, water, drainage and telecoms — secured or aspirational?
  • Planning path. The specific approvals required, the likely conditions and the realistic timetable.
  • Absorption. Who buys or leases the finished product, at what price, and in what order?
  • Exit liquidity. The buyer pool for raw land versus consented land versus built product is not the same.

Land rewards patience, but only when the patience is priced. The corridor story is real; the discipline is to separate the infrastructure that will be delivered from the infrastructure that is merely desired.
